\begin{remark}[Tightness and the moment vector]
Labor market tightness $\theta = v/u$ is omitted from the SMM moment vector
despite being the central state variable in the matching block.
The case for inclusion rests on persistence: $\rho(\theta)$ is not recoverable
from $\rho(u)$ and $\rho(v)$ alone, because it depends on the
cross-autocorrelations $\mathrm{corr}(u_t, v_{t-1})$ and
$\mathrm{corr}(v_t, u_{t-1})$, which are absent from our vector.
Since $\rho(\theta)$ carries identifying power primarily for the matching
elasticity $\alpha$---governing how tightness transmits to the job-finding
rate $f = \mu \theta^{1-\alpha}$---its exclusion is innocuous once $\alpha$
is calibrated externally.
Following \citet{PetrongoloandPissarides2001}, we set $\alpha = 0.5$,
so including $\theta$ would add near-collinear moments without independent
identifying content and risk inflating the condition number of the optimal
weighting matrix.
We nonetheless report the derived Shimer amplification ratio
$\sigma(\theta)/\sigma(z)$ in Table~\ref{tab:smm_moments} as a
diagnostic check on model-generated volatility.
\end{remark}
